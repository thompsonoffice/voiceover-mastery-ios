Part 1. iPhone (iOS)
Chapter 1.18. Phone, Messages, and Mail
============================================================

OVERVIEW
Calls, texts, and email are everyday VoiceOver tasks. Apple documents specific VoiceOver behavior for the Phone app, including how to answer and end a call with Magic Tap. Messages and Mail use the same general VoiceOver skills you already have: lists, text fields, the rotor, and actions. This chapter covers the official Phone details first, then practical, documented patterns for Messages and Mail without inventing app-specific gestures Apple does not list.


WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
- How to answer and end a call with a two-finger double tap.
- How the in-call keypad and call options work with VoiceOver.
- How to move through message and mail lists, and how to write and send.
- How notifications can announce new messages, and how to change that.


MAKE AND RECEIVE PHONE CALLS
1. With VoiceOver on, go to the Phone app.
2. To answer or end a call, double-tap the screen with two fingers.

That is the same Magic Tap gesture from Chapter 1.4. It is the official way to answer or hang up without hunting for a small button during a ringing phone.

When a call is established with VoiceOver on, the screen shows the numeric keypad by default, instead of showing call options.

- To display call options, select the Hide button in the lower-right corner, then double-tap.
- To display the numeric keypad again, select the Keypad button near the center of the screen, then double-tap.

If you need the speaker, mute, or add-call controls, use Hide to reveal call options, then swipe to the control you want and double-tap. Auto-select Speaker in Call, described in Chapter 1.15, can automatically switch to the speaker when you are not holding iPhone to your ear.

To place a call, use the Phone app's keypad, Recents, Contacts, or Favorites the way you use any other list or button: select, then double-tap. The keypad keys work with your usual typing style, but you are entering digits, not text.


MESSAGES
Apple does not publish a separate, complete VoiceOver gesture list just for Messages. Use the standard tools:

1. Open Messages. Swipe through the conversation list, or use Search if the app provides a search field.
2. Double-tap a conversation to open it.
3. Swipe through messages in the thread. A two-finger swipe down can read from the selected message onward, which is useful in a long thread.
4. To write, select the message field, double-tap, then type, use Braille Screen Input, or dictate.
5. In Braille Screen Input, a three-finger swipe up can send a message, according to Apple's Braille Screen Input page. Otherwise, select the Send button and double-tap.

If a conversation item offers extra actions, swipe up or down with the rotor on Actions, then double-tap. Do not assume every action name from one iOS version will match the next. Listen to what is offered on your phone.

New text messages may be read aloud when they arrive, even on the Lock Screen, because of VoiceOver's default notification verbosity (Chapter 1.16). You can change that in Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Verbosity > System Notifications if spoken incoming texts are a problem in public or during a meeting.


MAIL
Mail is also a list-and-detail app.

1. Open Mail and swipe through mailboxes or message lists. Headings and containers on the rotor can help in the mailbox list.
2. Double-tap a message to open it.
3. Use a two-finger swipe down to read the message from the current point, or move by lines and paragraphs with the rotor.
4. For messages in HTML, rotor items such as Headings, Links, and Landmarks can work the same way they do in Safari.
5. To reply or compose, activate the relevant button, double-tap each address or subject field you need to edit, and write in the body field.
6. Use the Edit rotor or Text Selection to revise, then activate Send.

Apple notes that VoiceOver can read PDFs in the Books and Files apps, including forms, tables, and lists. If a message has a PDF attachment, opening it in Files or Books may give you better structure than a brief preview. That is an official PDF feature, not a Mail-only claim.


PRACTICE EXERCISE
1. Open Phone, explore Recents or Contacts, and make sure you can find the keypad.
2. With a friend or a voicemail number you are willing to call, practice ending a call with a two-finger double tap. Do not practice this on an emergency line.
3. Open Messages, compose a message to yourself, and send it with the Send button.
4. Open Mail, read one message with a two-finger swipe down, and locate Reply without sending unless you intend to.
5. If incoming message announcements are too talkative, review Verbosity > System Notifications.


QUICK REFERENCE SUMMARY
- Phone: two-finger double tap answers or ends a call.
- During a call, the keypad is shown by default. Hide (lower right) shows call options. Keypad (near center) brings the keypad back.
- Messages and Mail use ordinary lists, text fields, reading gestures, and the Actions rotor.
- Braille Screen Input can send a message with a three-finger swipe up.
- VoiceOver may speak incoming texts even when iPhone is locked unless you change System Notifications verbosity.
- PDFs may be easier to read in Books or Files, where VoiceOver can describe forms, tables, and lists.
