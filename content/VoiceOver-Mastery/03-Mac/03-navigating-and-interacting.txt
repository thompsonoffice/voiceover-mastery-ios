Part 3. Mac (macOS)
Chapter 3.3. Navigating and Interacting with Areas
============================================================

OVERVIEW
Content in VoiceOver on Mac is organized hierarchically. Some items contain other items. Apple calls an item that contains nested items an area (such as a list of files) or a group (such as a toolbar of buttons).

By default, you must interact with an area or group before you can navigate to its items. That lets you skip a group, or stop and go inside it. This is the biggest conceptual difference from iPhone's usual swipe-through-everything habit, especially if your iPhone navigation style is Flat.


WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
- How to move the VoiceOver cursor and activate items, reviewed briefly.
- How to start and stop interacting with an area or group.
- How to recognize when interaction is required.
- How this compares to grouped navigation on iPhone.


MOVE AND ACTIVATE, AGAIN
- VO-Left Arrow or VO-Right Arrow moves the VoiceOver cursor.
- VO-Space bar performs the default action.

If nothing else is in that direction, VoiceOver can play a hollow sound. Change direction or interact with the current item if it is a group.


INTERACT WITH A GROUP
When the VoiceOver cursor moves to an area or group, VoiceOver indicates that you are on an item that can be interacted with.

- Press VO-Shift-Down Arrow to interact with the area or group.
- Press VO-Shift-Up Arrow to stop interacting and exit the area or group.

After you interact, you can navigate only within that area or group until you exit it. This is similar to moving into a group on iPhone with a two-finger swipe right, then out with a two-finger swipe left, if you use grouped navigation.

If you press VO-Right Arrow and seem to skip a whole toolbar or list, you probably need to interact first. If you get "stuck" inside a table or sidebar, press VO-Shift-Up Arrow to exit, then move on.


TRACKPAD EQUIVALENTS
If you use trackpad gestures (Chapter 3.6):

- Two-finger flick right starts interacting with the item in the VoiceOver cursor.
- Two-finger flick left stops interacting.

Those official trackpad gestures match the keyboard interact and exit commands.


PRACTICE EXERCISE
1. Open Finder, or a folder on the desktop.
2. Move with VO-Right Arrow until VoiceOver says you can interact with a list, toolbar, or group.
3. Press VO-Shift-Down Arrow, move among the items inside, then press VO-Shift-Up Arrow to exit.
4. Repeat in Safari or System Settings, which are full of groups.
5. If you get lost, press Escape or VO-Shift-Up Arrow until you are out of the current group, then VO-H if you have already learned the Help menu, or just keep exiting until you hear a familiar window.


QUICK REFERENCE SUMMARY
- VO-arrows move. VO-Space bar activates.
- Areas and groups must often be entered before you can move to the items inside.
- VO-Shift-Down Arrow interacts (goes in). VO-Shift-Up Arrow exits.
- After you interact, movement stays inside that group until you exit.
- On a trackpad, two-finger flick right goes in, and two-finger flick left goes out.
- This is the Mac cousin of grouped navigation on iPhone.
