VOICEOVER MASTERY: THE COMPLETE APPLE GUIDE
A book in SRTS: Screen Reader Training Suite
Part 7. Apps, Practice, and Sources
Chapter 7.2. A Practice Plan from Beginner to Power User
============================================================

OVERVIEW
This plan turns the book into a practice sequence. It is a suggestion, not a test. Spend more days on any step that still feels shaky. Skip a later platform chapter until you have the device in your hands.

The plan assumes iPhone is your first VoiceOver device. If you start on iPad or Mac, use the matching part, but still learn the shared ideas: move, activate, go back, pause, mute, Screen Curtain, and the rotor.


WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
- A beginner sequence for the first week.
- What "comfortable" means before you add power-user tools.
- How to add a second Apple device without starting over.


WEEK 1: GET ON, GET OFF, AND MOVE
Day 1. Turn VoiceOver on and off in Settings, with Siri, and with the Accessibility Shortcut (Chapter 1.1). Set the shortcut now.

Day 2. Use the VoiceOver Tutorial and VoiceOver Practice (Chapter 1.2). Practice swipe right, swipe left, double-tap, and a two-finger scrub until they are reliable.

Day 3. Explore the Home Screen, open and leave apps, and use the Item Chooser (Chapters 1.3 through 1.5).

Day 4. Learn mute and Screen Curtain, then immediately practice turning Screen Curtain off (Chapter 1.6). This is the day that prevents later panic.

Day 5. Practice Home, App Switcher, Control Center, and notifications for your iPhone model (Chapter 1.7).

Day 6. Learn the rotor. Use Speaking Rate and Headings (Chapter 1.8). Customize the rotor so those items are easy to find (Chapter 1.9).

Day 7. Rest, then repeat the gestures that still fail. Do not add typing yet if movement is still unreliable.


WEEK 2: TYPE, EDIT, AND COMMUNICATE
Days 8 and 9. Learn Standard Typing, then try Touch Typing if you want speed. Enable dictation if you want it (Chapters 1.10 and 1.12).

Day 10. Practice moving the insertion point and fixing a typo in Notes (Chapter 1.13).

Day 11. If you know braille, start Braille Screen Input in a note, not in an important message (Chapter 1.11). If you do not know braille, skip this day.

Day 12. Make a low-stakes Phone, Messages, and Mail practice: a message to yourself, a call you intend to end with Magic Tap (Chapter 1.18).

Day 13. Rearrange one app or learn Search from the Home Screen (Chapter 1.14). Adjust speaking rate and voice if needed (Chapter 1.15).

Day 14. Review troubleshooting once while everything still works (Chapter 1.25).


WEEK 3: THE WEB AND THE CAMERA
Days 15 and 16. Browse Safari by headings, links, and Reader View (Chapter 1.19).

Day 17. Try Maps in a familiar indoor or quiet outdoor place, not as street-crossing guidance (Chapter 1.20).

Day 18. Take a photo with Magic Tap and listen to Camera's viewfinder descriptions (Chapter 1.20).

Day 19. Try Live Recognition in a safe room, then turn it off (Chapter 1.21). Read the safety warning again.

Day 20. If you have a badly labeled app, try Screen Recognition for that app only (Chapter 1.22).

Days 21. Optional: connect a keyboard or a braille display (Chapters 1.23 and 1.24), or rest.


AFTER THE FIRST THREE WEEKS
Add power-user settings only when you feel the need: Activities, custom commands, custom labels, Quick Settings, punctuation groups (Chapters 1.16 and 1.17). Changing everything at once makes troubleshooting harder.

Then add other devices one at a time:

- iPad: Part 2, especially the Dock gesture and the two-tone versus three-tone system gestures.
- Mac: Part 3, starting with Command-F5, VO-arrows, VO-Space bar, and interact/exit. Do the official Mac tutorial.
- Apple Watch: Part 4, starting with the Digital Crown shortcut and the two-finger scrub.
- Apple TV: Part 5, starting with the Menu or Back triple-press and the two VoiceOver modes.
- Vision Pro: Part 6, starting with the official tutorial, All Commands, and next, previous, and activate pinches.

Chapter 7.1 can be added whenever you want a volunteer or OCR backup, after you already know Live Recognition and Magnifier.


WHAT "COMFORTABLE" MEANS
You do not need to memorize this whole book. You are comfortable when you can:

- Turn VoiceOver on and off without help.
- Move, activate, go back, and pause speech.
- Recover from mute and Screen Curtain.
- Use the rotor for speaking rate and at least one navigation type, such as headings.
- Type or dictate a short message, and fix a typo.
- Open Control Center or the equivalent on your device, and go Home.

Everything after that is speed and comfort, not a requirement.


PRACTICE EXERCISE
1. Circle, or note, which week you are actually in. Start there, not at the end of the book.
2. Pick one gesture that still fails and spend ten minutes in VoiceOver Practice, Keyboard Help, or the Vision Pro practice area.
3. Teach one recovery step (mute, Screen Curtain, or the Accessibility Shortcut) to a person in your home, so they can help without taking the phone away.


QUICK REFERENCE SUMMARY
- Week 1: on/off, move, activate, scrub, mute, Screen Curtain, Home, rotor.
- Week 2: typing, editing, messages, search, troubleshooting review.
- Week 3: Safari, Maps, Camera, Live Recognition, optional keyboard or braille.
- Add settings and extra devices later, one at a time.
- Comfort means you can start, stop, move, recover, use the rotor, and send a short message.
